**Q1.** *(Difficulty: Easy · Expert 12s / Avg 55s · DR 4.6)*

A shopkeeper marks the price of an article at 25% above its cost price and then offers a discount of 20% on the marked price. What is the profit or loss percentage on the cost price?

- (1) 5% loss
- ✅ (2) No profit, no loss
- (3) 5% profit
- (4) 1% profit

**Answer: (2) No profit, no loss**

**Solution:** Let CP = 100. Marked Price (MP) = 100 + 25% of 100 = 125. Selling Price (SP) = MP - 20% of MP = 125 - 0.20 x 125 = 125 - 25 = 100. Since SP = CP = 100, there is no profit and no loss. Option 1 ('5% loss'): Error — computing 25% - 20% = 5% and concluding a 5% loss without accounting for base differences. Option 3 ('5% profit'): Error — adding the two percentages directly: 25% - 20% = 5% profit, ignoring that discount applies to MP (not CP). Option 4 ('1% profit'): Error — computing 25 x 20 / 100 = 5, then 25 - 20 - 5 = 0 but rounding/misapplying as 1% profit.

**Q13.** *(Difficulty: Medium · Expert 20s / Avg 130s · DR 6.5)*

A train travels from Station X to Station Y at a speed of 60 km/h and returns at a speed of 90 km/h. What is the average speed for the entire journey?

- (1) 73 km/h
- ✅ (2) 72 km/h
- (3) 75 km/h
- (4) 78 km/h

**Answer: (2) 72 km/h**

**Solution:** For two equal-distance legs, average speed = 2v₁v₂ / (v₁ + v₂) = 2 × 60 × 90 / (60 + 90) = 10800 / 150 = 72 km/h.

Option 1 (73 km/h): Error — confusing harmonic mean with geometric mean. The geometric mean of 60 and 90 is √(60 × 90) = √5400 ≈ 73.5 km/h, which the student rounds to 73 km/h. The geometric mean is the correct 'middle' for multiplicative relationships (e.g., compound growth), not for averaging speeds over equal distances.

Option 3 (75 km/h): Error — using the arithmetic mean (60 + 90) / 2 = 75 km/h. This would only be correct if both legs were travelled for equal amounts of TIME, not equal distances. Since the train spends more time at the slower speed (60 km/h), the true average is pulled below 75 km/h.

Option 4 (78 km/h): Error — computing the 'speed-squared weighted mean' (v₁² + v₂²) / (v₁ + v₂) = (3600 + 8100) / 150 = 11700 / 150 = 78 km/h. This formula has no physical basis for this scenario; a student arrives at it by misremembering a velocity-addition rule.

**Q24.** *(Difficulty: Medium · Expert 22s / Avg 155s · DR 7.0)*

Five people P, Q, R, S, and T are seated in a straight row from left to right (positions 1 to 5). The following conditions hold: (i) R sits exactly in the middle. (ii) P sits immediately to the left of Q. (iii) S sits at one of the two ends. (iv) T sits to the right of R. Which of the following is definitely true?

- (1) S sits at the leftmost position
- ✅ (2) Q sits 2nd from the left
- (3) T sits immediately to the right of Q
- (4) P sits 4th from the left

**Answer: (2) Q sits 2nd from the left**

T must be > 3, so T in {4, 5} — both qualify. S must be at an end (1 or 5); position 1 is taken by P, so S = 5 and T = 4. Unique arrangement: P=1, Q=2, R=3, T=4, S=5. Checking the options: Option 2 (Q 2nd from left) — Q=2, TRUE. Option 1 (S leftmost) — S=5, not position 1, FALSE; error: assuming S is at the left end rather than determining which end. Option 3 (T immediately right of Q) — Q=2, T=4, not adjacent (position 3 lies between them), FALSE; error: confusing 'T to the right of R' with 'T adjacent to Q'. Option 4 (P 4th from left) — P=1, not 4, FALSE; error: placing the P-Q pair at positions 4-5 and failing to check constraint (iv).

**Q30.** *(Difficulty: Medium · Expert 30s / Avg 200s · DR 6.7)*

**Q50.** *(Difficulty: Hard · Expert 42s / Avg 350s · DR 8.3)*

A fair six-sided die is rolled up to 3 times. The rolling stops as soon as a 6 appears. You win Rs. 10 if a 6 appears on the 1st roll, Rs. 6 if a 6 first appears on the 2nd roll, and Rs. 3 if a 6 first appears on the 3rd roll. You win nothing if no 6 appears. What is your expected winning (to the nearest paisa)?

- (1) Rs. 3.50
- (2) Rs. 4.00
- ✅ (3) Rs. 2.85
- (4) Rs. 3.20

**Answer: (3) Rs. 2.85**

**Solution:** P(6 on roll 1) = 1/6. P(first 6 on roll 2) = (5/6)*(1/6) = 5/36. P(first 6 on roll 3) = (5/6)^2*(1/6) = 25/216. Expected winning = 10*(1/6) + 6*(5/36) + 3*(25/216) = 360/216 + 180/216 + 75/216 = 615/216 = 2.847... ≈ Rs. 2.85. Option 3 is correct. Distractor 1 (Rs. 3.50): student takes a simple average of the three payoffs (10+6+3)/3 = 19/3 ≈ 6.33, then halves it due to a rough 50% win probability estimate, getting ~3.50. Distractor 2 (Rs. 4.00): student assigns equal probability 1/6 to each of the three winning scenarios and zero to losing: E = (10+6+3)/6 + 0 = 19/6 ≈ 3.17, rounded up to 4.00, or computes 24/6=4 by an arithmetic slip. Distractor 4 (Rs. 3.20): student correctly computes probabilities for rolls 1 and 2 (total 1/6+5/36=11/36) but ignores roll 3, computing 10*(1/6)+6*(5/36) = 60/36+30/36 = 90/36 = 2.50, then adds an estimated 0.70 for roll 3, arriving at 3.20.
